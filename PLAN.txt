# Project Specification: SPY Daily Market Intelligence Platform

**Project name:** SPY Market Intelligence  
**Version:** 1.0 — Implementation Specification  
**Status:** Ready for delegation  
**Primary instrument:** SPY (SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust)  
**Forecast horizon:** Next 20 US trading sessions  
**Technology:** Python, Streamlit, PostgreSQL, scheduled data pipelines  
**Primary output:** Interactive daily market intelligence dashboard  
**Secondary outputs:** Daily reports, historical forecasts, backtesting results and alerts

---

# 1. Executive Summary

## 1.1 Project Objective

Build a production-quality, automated market intelligence platform that evaluates the US equity market through SPY and generates an evidence-based assessment of expected market conditions over the following 20 trading sessions.

The platform must:

1. Collect and validate financial market data automatically.
2. Calculate a comprehensive collection of technical, volatility, credit, breadth, liquidity and macroeconomic indicators.
3. Identify the prevailing market regime.
4. Evaluate whether conditions support bullish continuation, sideways consolidation or bearish correction.
5. Estimate forward 20-trading-day return distributions using historical evidence.
6. Generate an interpretable BUY / HOLD / REDUCE RISK classification.
7. Explain which indicators support or contradict the assessment.
8. Track daily changes in market conditions.
9. Store every forecast and compare it with subsequent actual market performance.
10. Present results in an interactive Streamlit dashboard.
11. Operate automatically after US market close, without requiring manual intervention.
12. Clearly distinguish verified data, model estimates, heuristic classifications and unavailable information.

**Critical design principle:** The platform must not blindly reproduce existing trading rules. It must calculate raw indicators independently and establish whether their predictive usefulness is supported by historical testing.

## 1.2 Intended Users

The primary user is an individual investor or market analyst who wants to monitor SPY and make more informed decisions about equity exposure over approximately one month.

The system is a decision-support tool, not an automated trading platform.

**Out of scope for Version 1:** Trade execution, brokerage integration, leveraged ETF allocation, individual stock recommendations and intraday algorithmic trading.

## 1.3 Primary User Questions

Every daily report must answer:

- Is the SPY market environment currently bullish, neutral or bearish?
- Is the current trend strengthening or weakening?
- Is the rally supported by broad market participation?
- Are credit and volatility markets signalling increased risk?
- What has changed since yesterday?
- What are the historical 20-day outcomes following similar conditions?
- What is the probability of a positive return, based on validated historical evidence?
- What is the plausible upside and downside range?
- Which indicators would cause the outlook to change?
- Does the evidence favour adding exposure, maintaining exposure or reducing risk?

# 4. Indicator Engine

Implement each indicator as an independent, documented and testable Python module.

Every indicator must return:

- Raw value
- Historical comparison
- Direction of change
- Normalized value, where applicable
- Interpretation
- Data freshness
- Data quality
- Explanation
- Historical predictive evidence, when available

## 4.1 Price and Momentum Indicators

Calculate:

- SMA20, SMA50, SMA100 and SMA200
- EMA20 and EMA50
- RSI(14)
- MACD(12,26,9)
- 5-day return
- 10-day return
- 20-day return
- 60-day return
- Distance from moving averages
- 20-day realized volatility
- ATR(14)
- ATR relative to historical median
- Maximum drawdown over rolling windows

Determine whether SPY is:

- Trending upward
- Consolidating
- Losing upward momentum
- Breaking down
- Recovering from a correction

Avoid using RSI above 70 as an automatic SELL signal. Interpret it in the context of trend and historical performance.

## 4.2 Market Breadth Indicators

Calculate:

- Percentage of constituents above SMA20
- Percentage above SMA50
- Percentage above SMA200
- Advance/decline ratio
- Cumulative advance/decline line
- New highs minus new lows
- RSP/SPY relative strength
- Breadth divergence from SPY

The system must account for historical constituent membership to avoid survivorship bias.

If point-in-time membership is unavailable, label the breadth series as a proxy or research limitation.

Identify conditions such as:

- Broadly supported rally
- Narrow leadership
- Improving participation
- Deteriorating participation
- Broad market weakness

## 4.3 Volatility Indicators

Calculate:

- VIX level
- VIX 5-day and 20-day changes
- VIX9D/VIX ratio
- VIX/VIX3M ratio
- VIX futures front-month/second-month ratio, if available
- SPY realized volatility
- Implied versus realized volatility
- ATR volatility regime
- Volatility percentile relative to historical data

Explicitly define whether each ratio represents contango or backwardation.

Volatility regime classifications:

- Low
- Normal
- Elevated
- High stress

Thresholds should initially be based on historical percentiles and subsequently validated.

## 4.4 Credit Indicators

Calculate:

- LQD/IEF ratio
- LQD/IEF SMA200
- LQD/IEF EMA20 and EMA50
- Percentage price oscillators
- HYG/IEF relative strength
- High-yield spread level
- High-yield spread 5-day change
- High-yield spread 20-day change
- Investment-grade spread trend

Detect:

- Improving credit conditions
- Stable credit conditions
- Emerging credit deterioration
- Severe risk aversion

Credit spreads must be interpreted using their own historical distribution rather than arbitrary universal thresholds.

## 4.5 Treasury Indicators

Calculate:

- Current yields by maturity
- Yield changes over 5 and 20 trading days
- Yield curve slopes
- Curve steepening or flattening
- Historical yield percentiles
- Rate volatility where data is available

Classify:

- Bull steepening
- Bear steepening
- Bull flattening
- Bear flattening
- Inverted yield curve

Do not automatically interpret falling yields as bullish. Their significance depends on inflation, growth expectations, credit conditions and the equity market reaction.

## 4.6 Sector Rotation

Track relative strength for:

- XLK — Technology
- XLF — Financials
- XLI — Industrials
- XLY — Consumer Discretionary
- XLP — Consumer Staples
- XLV — Healthcare
- XLU — Utilities
- XLE — Energy
- SMH — Semiconductors

Calculate sector performance over 5, 20 and 60 sessions.

Identify:

- Cyclical leadership
- Defensive leadership
- Technology concentration
- Broad participation
- Sector leadership reversals

# 5. Market Scoring Engine

## 5.1 Initial Score Architecture

Start with six indicator groups:

| Category | Initial weight |
|---|---:|
| Price and momentum | 25% |
| Market breadth | 25% |
| Credit conditions | 15% |
| Volatility and options | 15% |
| Liquidity and macro | 10% |
| Sector rotation | 10% |
| **Total** | **100%** |

These weights are initial research assumptions, not established predictive relationships.

## 5.2 Score Calculation

Each category produces a normalized score between -100 and +100.

The composite score is the weighted average of the six categories.

Initial descriptive classification:

| Composite score | Classification |
|---|---|
| +60 to +100 | Strong bullish |
| +20 to below +60 | Moderately bullish |
| -20 to below +20 | Neutral |
| -60 to below -20 | Moderately bearish |
| -100 to below -60 | Strong bearish |

The boundaries must be configurable.

The developer must test whether these categories have useful predictive power. If not, the classifications must be revised or removed.

## 5.3 Correlation Controls

Avoid double-counting highly correlated indicators.

For example:

- RSI, MACD and moving-average distance may measure similar momentum effects.
- VIX, realized volatility and ATR may respond to the same underlying stress.
- Several credit ratios may convey overlapping information.

Implement correlation analysis, feature redundancy reporting and sensitivity testing.

The dashboard must explain the contribution of each category to the composite score.

# 6. Twenty-Trading-Day Forecast Engine

This is the most important analytical component.

## 6.1 Forecast Targets

For each historical observation date, calculate:

1. SPY forward 20-session total return.
2. Maximum adverse excursion over the following 20 sessions.
3. Maximum favourable excursion.
4. Whether the final return is positive.
5. Whether a drawdown of at least 3%, 5% or 8% occurs.
6. Forward realized volatility.
7. Maximum drawdown within the forecast horizon.

Use split- and distribution-adjusted prices for total-return calculations.

Keep raw market prices separately for charting and execution-level comparisons.

## 6.2 Historical Analogue Model

Identify historical market conditions similar to the current state using:

- Trend
- Momentum
- Breadth
- Credit
- Volatility
- Treasury conditions
- Sector rotation

Normalize features using information available at the historical observation date.

Use a documented similarity method, such as standardized nearest neighbours.

For each forecast, show:

- Number of comparable historical observations
- Median forward return
- Mean forward return
- Positive-return frequency
- 10th and 90th percentile outcomes
- Historical drawdown frequency
- Effective sample size
- Confidence limitations

Overlapping 20-day windows must not be treated as statistically independent.

## 6.3 Predictive Models

Develop and compare:

**Baseline A:** Historical unconditional 20-day SPY return distribution.

**Baseline B:** Simple price trend model.

**Model C:** Historical analogue model.

**Model D:** Regularized logistic regression for positive-return probability.

**Model E:** Gradient-boosted model, only if it demonstrates robust out-of-sample improvement.

Complex models must not automatically replace simpler models.

Select models based on predictive calibration, robustness and out-of-sample performance.

## 6.4 Scenario Probabilities

Estimate:

- Probability of positive 20-day return
- Probability of a negative 20-day return
- Probability of a decline of at least 3%
- Probability of a decline of at least 5%
- Expected or median return
- Predictive return interval

Also classify outcomes into mutually exclusive categories, for example:

- Bullish: return above +2%
- Sideways: return between -2% and +2%, inclusive
- Bearish: return below -2%

These thresholds must be configurable.

All three scenario probabilities must sum to 100%.

If historical evidence is insufficient or model calibration is poor, display **Insufficient evidence** rather than fabricated probabilities.

## 6.5 Forecast Confidence

Confidence should reflect:

- Data completeness
- Model calibration
- Out-of-sample stability
- Number of effective historical analogues
- Distance from previously observed market conditions
- Agreement or disagreement between models

Confidence must not be based solely on how many indicators are bullish.

# 7. Historical Backtesting Framework

## 7.1 Requirements

Backtest across multiple market regimes, ideally including:

- 2011 US debt-ceiling volatility
- 2015–2016 market turbulence
- 2018 tightening and correction
- 2020 pandemic crash
- 2022 inflation and tightening cycle
- 2023–2025 market environments
- 2026 observations available at evaluation time

The exact start date depends on historical data availability.

## 7.2 Point-in-Time Integrity

This requirement is mandatory.

For every historical forecast, the model must use only information that was available at that point in time.

Controls must include:

- No future prices in features
- No full-sample normalization
- No future constituent membership
- No unreleased economic data
- No future-revised macroeconomic values presented as original observations
- No training labels that overlap into the evaluation period
- Appropriate purging and embargoes for overlapping forecast windows

Historical macroeconomic release dates and revisions must be considered.

## 7.3 Walk-Forward Validation

Use expanding-window or rolling-window training.

Example:

- Train on the initial historical period.
- Predict the next evaluation period.
- Advance the training window.
- Retrain according to a fixed schedule.
- Repeat through the available history.

Keep a final untouched holdout period for model selection confirmation.

The developer must document all training and testing date ranges.

## 7.4 Evaluation Metrics

Measure:

- Directional accuracy
- Balanced accuracy where appropriate
- Brier score
- Log loss
- Probability calibration
- Mean absolute error for return estimates
- Prediction interval coverage
- Maximum drawdown prediction quality
- Performance across different volatility regimes
- Stability across time periods

Compare every model with simple baselines.

## 7.5 Strategy Simulation

Separately evaluate whether forecast-based allocation decisions would have improved risk-adjusted outcomes.

Possible simulated states:

- Full SPY exposure
- Reduced SPY exposure
- Cash or Treasury-bill allocation

Include:

- Transaction costs
- Slippage assumptions
- Dividends
- Financing and cash returns
- Rebalance timing
- Turnover
- Maximum drawdown
- Sharpe ratio
- Sortino ratio
- CAGR
- Buy-and-hold comparison

Do not use backtest profitability as the only model-selection criterion.

The platform must distinguish forecast quality from strategy profitability.

# 8. BUY / HOLD / REDUCE RISK Decision Layer

The system should generate an actionable interpretation without pretending to know the user's full portfolio.

## 8.1 Outputs

- **BUY BIAS:** Conditions historically favour maintaining or increasing equity exposure.
- **HOLD:** Evidence is mixed or does not justify changing exposure.
- **REDUCE RISK:** Conditions suggest elevated downside risk.

Avoid presenting these as guaranteed recommendations.

## 8.2 Decision Logic

The initial decision engine should consider:

- Composite score
- Calibrated positive-return probability
- Estimated downside risk
- Current volatility regime
- Credit deterioration
- Market breadth
- Model confidence

The exact decision thresholds must be developed and validated during the research phase.

## 8.3 Explainability

Every classification must contain:

1. Three strongest supporting indicators.
2. Three strongest opposing indicators, where present.
3. Important changes from yesterday.
4. Conditions that would invalidate the current assessment.
5. Historical evidence supporting the conclusion.
6. Known data and model limitations.

Do not generate unsupported commentary.

A language model may optionally rewrite structured findings into readable prose, but it must not invent market data, probabilities or explanations.

# 10. Daily Automation

## 10.1 Schedule

Run after the US market closes, using an exchange-aware calendar.

Suggested process:

1. Wait for the scheduled post-close collection window.
2. Download market data.
3. Validate timestamps and completeness.
4. Update the database.
5. Calculate indicators.
6. Calculate the composite score.
7. Generate the 20-day forecast.
8. Generate the daily interpretation.
9. Save the forecast permanently.
10. Refresh the dashboard's published data.
11. Generate the daily report.
12. Send alerts when required.

Use America/New_York timezone rules so daylight-saving changes do not shift the intended market-relative schedule.

The scheduler must account for provider publication delays.

## 10.2 Failure Handling

If a source fails:

- Retry using exponential backoff.
- Avoid duplicate data insertion.
- Record the error.
- Preserve the last valid observation.
- Clearly mark stale data.
- Do not generate a new forecast from incomplete mandatory inputs.
- Alert the administrator after repeated failures.

The pipeline must be idempotent.

## 10.3 Model Retraining

Daily forecasts should not require full model retraining.

Suggested initial schedule:

- Daily: Data refresh, indicator calculations, forecast generation.
- Weekly: Data quality review and performance monitoring.
- Monthly: Candidate model retraining and validation.
- Quarterly: Full research review and potential model promotion.

New models must pass validation before replacing the production model.

# 11. Database Design

Recommended production database: PostgreSQL.

Required logical tables:

### market_prices

Stores OHLCV, adjusted prices, instruments and timestamps.

### macro_observations

Stores yields, spreads, economic indicators, release dates and revisions.

### breadth_observations

Stores constituent-level and aggregated market breadth.

### indicator_values

Stores calculated indicators and calculation versions.

### composite_scores

Stores category scores and overall market scores.

### forecasts

Stores immutable daily forecasts, probabilities, model versions and prediction intervals.

### forecast_outcomes

Stores realized forward returns and drawdowns after the forecast horizon completes.

### backtest_runs

Stores backtest configurations, model versions and evaluation results.

### data_quality_events

Stores missing data, stale observations and validation failures.

### pipeline_runs

Stores scheduler execution status, duration and errors.

### model_registry

Stores model versions, training windows, feature sets, metrics and promotion status.

### configuration_versions

Stores scoring weights, thresholds and decision rules used for each forecast.

All historical forecasts must remain reproducible after subsequent model or configuration changes.

- Restrict database permissions.

Monitor:

- Pipeline success rate
- Data freshness
- Data-provider errors

# 20. Definition of Done

The project is complete when:

**Every US trading day, after the required market data becomes available, the system automatically produces a current SPY market assessment covering the following 20 trading sessions.**

The user can open a secure Streamlit dashboard and see:

- Current SPY market conditions.
- Raw indicator readings.
- Changes since the previous session.
- Market breadth, momentum, volatility, credit and macroeconomic conditions.
- A transparent composite assessment.
- Historically grounded forward-return estimates.
- Downside-risk estimates.
- BUY / HOLD / REDUCE RISK interpretation.
- Evidence supporting and contradicting the conclusion.
- Historical forecasts and their actual outcomes.
- Model validation results.
- Data freshness and system health.

The system must be maintainable, reproducible and capable of being evaluated objectively as new market outcomes become available.

**Final principle:** The platform's purpose is not to generate a confident trading signal every day. Its purpose is to measure market conditions accurately, quantify uncertainty and identify when the evidence is strong enough to support a change in SPY exposure.
